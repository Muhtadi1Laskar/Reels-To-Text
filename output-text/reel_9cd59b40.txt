These are my five-star history books. The Silk Roads, A New History of the World by Peter
Frankopan. The Silk Road is arguably the most important piece of world history that we have.
It connected the Eastern and Western worlds. It expanded religion, political beliefs, trade,
commerce. It pushed forward cultural advancement and is the true link of the Eastern and Western
worlds that we have come to know today. Battle Cry of Freedom by James McPherson. This is a
masterpiece. This will tell you the history of the commerce, the society, the people,
the politics, the battles that shaped the history of the Civil War. Both The Sleepwalkers,
How Europe Went to War in 1914 by Christopher Clark, and The War That Ended Peace by Margaret
McMillan are five-star pre-World War I narratives on the history, the diplomacy, the politics of the
decisions and the leaders that led Europe to war in 1914. They are masterclass accounts of the times
leading up to 1914 and have been absolutely incredible to read, full of so much information
and books that I think if you're looking to learn about this period, you would absolutely love.
The Holocaust by Lawrence Reese. For such a heavy topic, Reese does a masterful job of highlighting the
pre-Holocaust history, the history as the Holocaust happened, the post-Holocaust history, the impact on
society and the world after the events of the Holocaust during and after World War II. Alan
Taylor's books on the American Revolution and the Civil War are both one-volume pieces that are
fantastic. I do truly believe that if push came to shove, these books could stand alone and represent
these genres in a fantastic manner. Taylor writes about the histories of the American Revolution and
the Civil War in a thematic way. Throughout the book, you're focused on one thing at a time. He's not
confusing you, going back and forth through timelines and interweaving his story throughout the book.
It is a fantastic way to tell history. It's the way that I prefer to read and learn about history.
And they're not massively huge. They're awesome to read and extremely floppy off the shelf, which
we love here. How the Word is Passed by Clint Smith. This is a book on America's relationship with
the history of slavery. He visits different areas in America and Africa. Highlights how human history
struggles with the retelling and acceptance of the history of slavery. Smith's prose throughout this
book is wonderful, poetic, beautiful, and extremely accessible for anybody that is looking to better
understand America's relationship with slavery. How Europe Underdeveloped Africa by Walter Rodney.
The textual retelling of the colonialism of Africa at the hands of the European powers.
This is extremely important history. Rodney does this in a wonderfully narrative way.
Absolute five stars. Wouldn't be a video of mine without a new world begins by Jeremy Popkin.
This is the most authoritative account on the French Revolution that there is available
in book. Popkin has studied French history for over 50 years and a new world begins is an
authoritative piece on French revolutionary history.
